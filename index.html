Dodge Smasher

2018AndroidShipped

A minimalist one-button action roguelike. Control an orb, hit enemies and dodge their attacks at the same time — time slows while you do, giving you room to plan.

Post-mortem

Started as a passion project, fun but unpolished, then shelved when life got in the way. A year later, between jobs, I roped in an old colleague for art and put in close to two months to launch it (formerly known as Impulse). Inspiration came from a lot of places, most notably Fowlst, plus years of playing roguelikes and action games.

Pidgen Racer

2015WindowsAcademic · Team

Futuristic hovercraft racer with procedurally generated tracks. A tribute to WipEout and roguelikes — the track is never the same twice, with adaptive difficulty.

Post-mortem

Very experimental, which suited the technical demands of the academic project it was built for. Combining roguelike track and world generation with hovercraft physics, we settled on an oct-tree approach to assembling track pieces, varying banking, width and pitch to tune difficulty and game feel.

Framework

2013WindowsLudum Dare 26

The original: top-down shipbuilding twin-stick shooter. Pick parts, assemble a ship, blast away. Solo project, built in a weekend for the Minimalism theme.

Post-mortem

One of my more solid efforts. It wasn't the most minimal — except for the aesthetics, which suited my abilities — but I was more enamoured with the idea than I should have been, and Ludum Dare was a good place to test it. The difficulty spikes are what make it janky, and enemies tend to surprise you off screen, which feels unfair. Lessons to learn.

Exit Fear

2013WindowsGlobal Game Jam

First-person survival horror. Sneak through a run-down office and reach the exit before the horrors catch you. Team project, 48 hours.

Built for GGJ 2013, theme "heartbeat" — and my first fully fleshed-out 3D game in Unity. Audience reception to the first few minutes was great, though the later level design could have been better. 48 hours left us little time to playtest and polish; we should have done more of it. Movmote

2012Flash · WebMIT GAMBIT

A top-down meditative puzzle game. Guide a glowing entity across a network of paths — its only abilities are to brake and change direction — with soothing visuals and soundtrack.

Post-mortem

Built during the MIT-GAMBIT Summer Program 2012 as a single-player research game that analyses players through abstract metaphor. Beyond doing it in Boston, at MIT no less, the value was working in a specialised team where everyone held a defined role. As the programmer it gave me a chance to lead on the engineering side and build designer tools too.

Bait

2013WindowsAcademic · Team

Two-player co-operative top-down action. Work as a team to carry the monster's head to the exit while evading the monster homing in on it — keep passing it between you.

Post-mortem

I worked as programmer and co-designer. A key aim was exploring design patterns behind co-operative play. It was also one of my most challenging dev experiences: designs changed constantly, right up to the last moment, to serve the academic research goals. In my opinion we didn't land a quality product — but the experience was enlightening.

LeVillain

2012WindowsLudum Dare 25

Top-down real-time strategy. Play a villainous summoner conquering the countryside — summon troops, subjugate fortresses, terrorise the locals. Solo project.

Post-mortem

My first Ludum Dare, theme "You are the villain", done in about 24 hours — other commitments left me half the usual time, and army reservice was waiting at the end of it. Overall too complex: I fell into the design-complexity trap, which made it hard to teach the player anything, and it ended up all over the place. A good exploration of ideas even so.

Magnet Jones

2011WindowsExperimental Gameplay Project

Top-down physics action. Guide a greedy explorer whose mystical orbs attract treasure and repel enemies through a series of dungeons. Solo project, about a week of work.

Post-mortem

Built for the Experimental Gameplay Project (the folks behind World of Goo), theme "Mash-Up" — so I melded action with physics puzzles, which was fun to play with in its own right: the repelling particles made majestic patterns. I added a health system tied to collected treasure, Sonic-style. As a designer, though, I didn't make it fun enough; the mechanics were interesting but the level design didn't support them.

About

I'm Victor Chua. I spent my early years making games — jams, academic projects, a research stint at MIT-GAMBIT, and one shipped mobile title — and I now work in tech as a software engineer, across front-end, UI/UX and interactive web.

The through-line is the same either way: build the smallest thing that proves the idea, ship it, write down what broke. Most of the post-mortems on this page are notes to my past self.

I still take on interactive web experiences for events. If that's something you need, drop me a message.
